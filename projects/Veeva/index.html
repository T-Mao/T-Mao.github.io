Veeva Systems

Platform quality, release validation, and test automation for Vault infrastructure.

Making platform changes ready to release

At Veeva Systems, I work as an Associate Software Engineer in Test, Infrastructure. My responsibilities cover the Durable Queue Framework, FileManager and FSA, and the Configuration Framework. These are platform services whose behavior matters to the teams and applications that depend on them.

My work combines release validation, defect investigation, and automation. I look for the situations in which a feature works in isolation but fails across an upgrade, a boundary condition, or a sequence of operations. Identifying those gaps and making them reproducible is the part of engineering I enjoy most.

Keeping the automation pipeline dependable

I have helped build the queue automation framework and contributed automated queue tests to the existing pipeline. I maintain queue automation and triage pipelines, investigate failed runs, and follow up on fixes. I work closely with the Hyderabad team to keep the queue automation pipeline stable and reliable.

That work extends beyond adding tests. A pipeline needs to remain useful as features and releases change. I take ownership of the investigation and follow-through that let the team understand a failure and decide what needs attention.

Asking questions about the design

Testing also gives me a reason to examine the proposed behavior itself. When a design leaves a failure mode unresolved, an expectation unclear, or a workflow difficult to validate, I raise the concern with the developers and work through it with them.

I want the eventual test to check an agreed behavior, rather than turn an ambiguous assumption into an automated assertion. Those conversations connect design decisions with the evidence we will need at release time.

What I contribute

Across the 25R3, 26R1, and 26R2 release cycles, my work has included:

  • Validating queue isolation and metadata behavior, and contributing to the associated automation.
  • Testing file staging APIs and SDK workflows, including changes involving performance, rate limits, and service behavior.
  • Supporting upgrade testing and release readiness across platform changes.
  • Maintaining queue automation pipelines, triaging failed runs, and improving UI automation stability.
  • Validating configuration changes and security hardening with the responsible engineering teams.

The common thread is ownership of the evidence behind a release decision: what was exercised, what failed, whether the failure can be reproduced, and what should be covered after a fix. A useful defect report gives an engineer a clear path from symptoms to a failing scenario. A useful automated test keeps that scenario visible in later releases.

Recognition

At my first Veeva anniversary, my manager, Timmy Ma Kwan, described me as “one of the key drivers behind our queue automation framework.” His feedback highlighted my automated queue coverage, ownership of pipeline maintenance, and collaboration with the Hyderabad team. He also recognized my work delivering Vault Message Isolation with a peer, emphasizing attention to detail, a meticulous approach, and commitment to quality.

In October 2025, I received Veeva Systems’ QA Innovation Certificate.

In September 2026, I was nominated for the Veeva 26R2 Platform Outstanding Contributor Award. The nomination recognized my work during the Vault Message Isolation rollout: identifying critical, feature-breaking defects through extensive release validation, helping prevent issues from reaching downstream environments, and contributing to the project’s automation framework while continuing to support other SET responsibilities.

That recognition matters to me because it reflects the work I want to be known for: careful investigation, follow-through, and a willingness to keep asking questions when the expected behavior and the observed behavior do not line up.
