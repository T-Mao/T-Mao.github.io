Graphic Design & Art Portfolio

Drawings, paintings, event materials, and original digital sticker collections.



A visual practice alongside engineering

I studied introductory and advanced graphic design at Arcadia High School and worked with Photoshop, Illustrator, and InDesign on illustration, typography, and print layouts. I also practiced traditional drawing and painting.

This collection brings together observational studies, school and community design work, and digital stickers. I enjoy the differences between them: a portrait asks for patient looking, an event poster asks for hierarchy, and a chat sticker asks for an expression that reads immediately.

Drawing and painting

Still Life: Pear (2024)

A pencil study of a pear, drawn from life. I concentrated on the transition from highlight to shadow and on keeping the fruit’s volume distinct from the cast shadow. Returning to still-life drawing in 2024 was a way to practice close observation without the speed or undo button of a digital tool.

The Future City (2017)

I imagined a city raised above water and vegetation, with bridges connecting its different districts. The painting combines dense architecture with waterfalls and open landscape to depict a future where the built environment makes room for nature. I spent more than a month developing the piece and experimenting with brushwork and spray-painting. It was selected among the Top 100 works in the 2017 Future Art China International Competition and exhibited in China and at Lincoln Center in New York.

Tibet: Digital Collage (2018)

This collage grew out of my travels to Tibet. I combined personal photography and graphic layers in Photoshop and Illustrator, bringing landscapes, architecture, and cultural details into one composition. The challenge was to preserve a sense of place while moving beyond a single photograph. It was selected for Arcadia High School’s annual art exhibition and school display.

Male Portrait Study (2017)

A graphite profile study focused on the relationships between the forehead, nose, lips, and chin. I used changes in value to describe the face and neck instead of relying on a strong outline everywhere. The study helped me see how a small shift in proportion can change the character of a portrait.

Female Portrait Study (2017)

A companion graphite portrait with a different pose and softer transitions across the face. I worked on the angle of the head, the placement of the features, and the contrast between hair and skin. Comparing the two portraits was useful practice in adapting the same medium to different forms.

Hand Gesture Studies (2017)

These sketches examine hands from several angles and in different poses. I broke each form into larger shapes before working on fingers, knuckles, and shadows. The collection records repeated attempts at a difficult subject: a gesture needs to read clearly before the small anatomical details can help it.

Skull Studies (2017)

Three views of the skull, drawn to understand the structure beneath a portrait. The eye sockets, cheekbones, and jaw create changes in plane that are easier to study here than beneath skin. Working across several angles helped me connect those structures instead of treating each drawing as an unrelated silhouette.

Plaster Cast Studies (2017)

Studies of an eye, a nose, a bust of Voltaire, and an anatomical head cast. These exercises isolate form and light: the subject does not move, and its surface gives fewer distractions than a live model. I practiced finding major planes first, then using value transitions to describe their depth.

Early Studies: Still Life and Figure (2014)

These pages include early still-life, cast, and figure exercises from 2014. I keep them in the portfolio because they show the practice behind the later work: simple shapes, observed proportions, and repeated studies of the same problem. They are an honest starting point for the visual skills I continued to develop.

Graphic design and print

Orchesis Dance Company: Recruitment Poster

A recruitment poster for Orchesis’s 2020 to 2021 auditions. I worked with the company on promotional material and developed a layout that balances an energetic visual style with several kinds of information: auditions, workshops, meetings, and eligibility. The poster needed to attract interest while helping a student find the next action to take.

Dance Day: Two-Sided Flyer (2020)

The front presents Dance Day 2020 through a bright palette and a prominent title. The reverse carries the registration form. Designing both sides meant switching between promotion and utility: one side invites someone to participate, and the other gives them a clear way to sign up. I worked on hierarchy, typography, and a print-ready layout.

Social Science: Classroom Poster Series (2019)

A four-part series about cognitive, communication, historical-thinking, and passion-inspired skills. I used a related layout and color system to make the pieces belong together, while giving each topic its own emphasis. The posters were used in school classrooms and display areas. The task was to organize several statements without losing the larger message.

Questions for Grades 6 to 9 (2019)

An educational poster built around question words and ideas such as change, shape, and perspective. I used varied lettering and a playful palette to give the text movement while retaining a clear grouping. The design was made for a younger school audience, which required a different tone from the more formal classroom series.

Air Pollution Awareness Poster (2019)

A public-awareness composition combining a person, industrial imagery, and a direct call to stop air pollution. I worked with photo editing and contrasting visual layers to connect an environmental issue with a human subject. The challenge was to create an immediate focal point while keeping the message readable.

Speech Follies: Event Poster (2019)

My design was selected for Arcadia High School’s Speech & Debate showcase. I built a theatrical scene around a speaker, spotlights, audience silhouettes, and scattered punctuation, with the title arranged vertically. Photoshop and Illustrator let me combine illustration and typography into one composition. The finished poster was printed and displayed around campus to promote the event.

Speech Follies: Event Program (2020)

A cover and interior spread for the 2020 event program. I organized schedules, names, and event information through a consistent typographic hierarchy and carried a related visual language across the booklet. Compared with a poster, the program demanded more attention to sustained readability and the relationships between multiple pages.

Georgia O’Keeffe: Bookmark and Invitation (2019)

A MoMA-inspired exhibition design exercise, pairing a bookmark with a cut-and-fold invitation. I used a shared type system and layout to connect the two formats, then considered how the invitation would reveal its imagery when handled. It was a way to study an identity across objects with different sizes and physical interactions.

Inspirational Quote Poster (2018)

A typography and image exercise built around the idea of doing one’s best today. I emphasized the final word through scale, color, and a contrasting image treatment. The work was selected for school display. It helped me practice making the reading order clear before adding decorative detail.

P!nk: Music Poster (2018)

A personal music poster featuring P!nk, combining a photographic portrait with expressive lettering and layered color. Music has long been one of my interests, and this piece let me work with a more energetic visual voice. I focused on keeping the performer recognizable while using the surrounding type to set the mood.

Orchesis: Hip-Hop Class Flyer (2018)

A flyer advertising an open hip-hop class, with a diagonal layout and a type treatment influenced by the subject. I worked with the dance company’s promotional needs and revised the design around its event information. This was early practice in making a piece for a real organizer rather than only for a classroom brief.

Annual Art Exhibition: Official Poster (2020)

The official poster for Arcadia High School’s 3rd Annual Art Exhibition, held in March and April 2020. I made “Art” the dominant element, then used contrasting angles, patterns, and a smaller information panel to organize the exhibition details. The poster brings several visual styles together, reflecting the range of work being shown. It was a chance to apply my graphic design training to a school event I also cared about as an artist.

Principles of Design (2018)

Eight small compositions exploring balance, contrast, emphasis, movement, pattern, rhythm, unity, and variety. Rather than explaining each principle only in words, I used shapes, repeated elements, and color to demonstrate them. Keeping the studies together on one page made it easier to compare how different visual rules change a composition.

ULike: Logo Exploration (2019)

Six logo directions for a fictional bath-products brand, created in an advanced graphic design course. I explored different relationships between lettering and symbols, from compact marks to more expressive wordmarks. Working on several alternatives helped me avoid settling on the first idea and consider what each direction would communicate about the same brand.

Clean Campus: Environmental Sticker (2019)

A campus campaign sticker featuring a superhero and the message “Let’s Pick It Up, Apaches!” The limited space called for a strong silhouette, a short line of text, and a shape that could stand on its own. I wanted the reminder to feel energetic and school-specific rather than like another instruction sign.

Digital illustration and stickers

Crocodile Character Stickers (2021)

Two WeChat sticker collections featuring an expressive crocodile character. The monochrome drawings rely on posture, facial expression, and accompanying text to communicate a reaction at chat size. Publishing the sets made me think about how a character stays recognizable across many moods and how a small image can carry a complete joke.

Fluffy Monster Stickers (2021)

A WeChat sticker collection built around colorful, round monster characters. These use a different visual language from the crocodile sets: simple shapes, expressive eyes, and a broader palette. Designing for messages taught me to test whether an expression still reads when the artwork is reduced to a very small size.

What I carry into product design

These projects taught me to revise around a purpose. In a drawing, that might mean simplifying the values so the form reads. In a poster, it might mean giving the event details enough room. In an app, it often means making the main action clearer and the secondary information quieter.

The media differ, but the habit is the same: observe carefully, decide what matters most, and keep working until the details support it.
