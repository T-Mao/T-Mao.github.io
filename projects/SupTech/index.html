SupTech Apps

Two Flutter apps connecting customers, technicians, and the service workflow.

Building both sides of a service business

At SupTech, I built two Flutter applications from the ground up: a Consumer App for requesting installation and repair services, and a Technician App for accepting and completing that work. I took responsibility for the mobile product from requirements and interface design through implementation, testing, and submission to the App Store and Google Play.

The challenge was larger than building two sets of screens. Each app represented one side of the same service workflow. A customer’s order, a technician’s acceptance, a status update, a message, and a payment all needed to tell a consistent story.

Two apps, one connected workflow

From left to right: (1) The Consumer App (C-end), allowing customers to create and track repair orders; (2) The Technician App (T-end), enabling technicians to pick up tasks and communicate in real time.

The Consumer App supports account access, service requests, product and fault details, photo uploads, location selection, payment, order tracking, chat, and feedback. I designed these steps around a practical question: what information does a technician need before arriving, and how can a customer provide it without a complicated form?

The Technician App supports nearby orders, acceptance, scheduling, customer communication, and service progress. Actions such as departing, beginning service, and completing a job make the workflow explicit. The app also includes work and income views and account setup for payouts.

My technical work

I used Flutter for the shared iOS and Android interfaces, with Provider and Riverpod in different modules. I integrated authentication, order APIs, chat, maps, location services, and Firebase Cloud Messaging. Handling permissions and location behavior across both platforms required attention to the differences beneath a shared interface.

The backend evolved during development. Early workflows used Firebase and Node.js functions; later integrations used Java APIs and MySQL. I worked with the backend teams to map the mobile screens to those APIs and clarify changes to business rules.

I integrated Stripe payment flows for customers and Stripe Connect account and payout workflows for technicians. The important product detail was keeping payment state understandable alongside order state, including delayed payouts and partial-refund scenarios.

Both apps also included an AI-assisted troubleshooting chat. I worked on the conversation flow and prompts, turning product and fault information into a sequence of troubleshooting questions and a route toward requesting service. The chat was integrated into the same product and service-request context as the rest of the app.

I organized English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese content and handled store listings, beta builds, and release submissions for both apps.

Work beyond implementation

I gathered requirements with stakeholders, documented workflows with mind maps and swimlane diagrams, and translated ambiguous requests into development tasks. I helped coordinate work with internal and external technical teams and supported onboarding for new colleagues. I also contributed to the company website and wrote materials that explained how to use the apps.

This role taught me to keep asking about the entire workflow. A screen can look finished while an order transition remains undefined. A feature can work on one platform while a permission path fails on the other. Building both apps gave me a close view of those dependencies and made testing the handoffs as important as testing the individual features.
