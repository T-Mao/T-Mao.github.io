LifeTune App

A team prototype exploring clearer daily health dashboards.

Less data to interpret, a clearer next step

LifeTune was a team project from January to March 2024 exploring how an iOS health dashboard could make diet, activity, and sleep information easier to understand. We developed interactive designs and a SwiftUI prototype with mock data.

I contributed to user research, competitive analysis, storyboards, wireframes, SwiftUI screens, and project coordination. We used the prototype to make the proposed experience concrete for participants.

What we heard

Our research included people who already tracked their health, existing app users, technical participants, and people wary of complex interfaces. A recurring concern was the effort of interpreting a crowded dashboard or repeatedly entering data.

We used that feedback to focus on a daily overview, small prompts, and visible progress. Participants also raised questions about privacy and control over data, which informed our discussion of what a future implementation would need.

From layouts to an interactive prototype

I helped translate the research into storyboards and wireframes, then built core screens and navigation in SwiftUI. Mock steps, meal, and activity data made it possible to demonstrate the flows without a live backend or HealthKit integration.

The earlier prototype helped us examine layout and navigation before committing to the visual treatment.

The later design introduced a mock-data “Health Score” to explore how a summary could work in the dashboard. It was an interface concept with no clinical validation. User tests informed revisions to menus, colors, and the daily check-in, meal logging, and progress flows.

The lesson in the prototype

I helped organize milestones and consolidate findings so design decisions stayed connected to the research. The prototype made discussions concrete: participants could respond to a screen and a task instead of an abstract feature list.

It also exposed a useful design tradeoff: a single summary can be easier to scan while hiding how it was calculated. The prototype helped me ask more precise questions about which details a summary should show and how a user can understand its basis.
