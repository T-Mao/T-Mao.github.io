Arcadia High School

Early foundations in programming, visual design, and academic exploration.

Where programming and design first met

At Arcadia High School, I studied programming alongside graphic design and traditional art. I graduated as Salutatorian with a 3.97 GPA, earned 5s in AP Computer Science A and AP Calculus AB, and scored 790 in SAT Mathematics Level II and 800 in Chinese with Listening.

The classes gave me a foundation, but the projects were what made these subjects personal. I liked making things that someone could use or see: a small game, a promotional poster, or a piece for an exhibition.

Designing for a real audience

I completed both introductory and advanced graphic design coursework, using Photoshop and Illustrator to practice composition, color, typography, and print layout. My design was selected for the school’s Speech & Debate poster, and I created the official poster for the Annual Art Exhibition. A Tibet-inspired digital collage was also selected for the exhibition.

Those projects brought constraints that classroom exercises sometimes leave open. An event poster needs to attract attention and make the date, location, and purpose easy to find. I learned to present drafts, work with feedback, and bring a design to completion for its intended audience. The art portfolio contains the finished pieces.

Learning to code by making things

AP Computer Science A and the programming club introduced me to structured programming and problem-solving. Outside class, I experimented with Python games, including Snake, and small mobile app ideas.

One project was a timed math game for my cousin. It combined questions of increasing difficulty with a leaderboard to make practice feel more like play. I also experimented with an anti-theft phone feature and tilt-based games. These early projects made the feedback loop immediate: build an idea, let someone try it, then discover what needs to change.

A foundation I still use

The connection between visual design and programming has stayed with me. Drawing trained me to notice details; design taught me to organize them; programming let me turn them into something interactive. High school was where I started exploring that combination, well before building and releasing my own apps.
